Europe Can Be Liked · ECBL

Erasmus+ Project

An Erasmus+ project on European citizenship involving Italy, Germany, Latvia and Poland.

About the ECBL Project

"Europe Can Be Liked" (ECBL) is an Erasmus+ cooperation project for adult education. It contrasts Euroscepticism — especially among the younger generation — by drawing on the memories and experience of senior citizens.

By combining historical knowledge with modern teaching methods, the project promotes awareness of the European Union and strengthens a shared European identity through workshops, publications, public debates and study visits.

Duration 2025 – 2027 24 months
Action Erasmus+ KA220-ADU Adult Education Partnership
Priority Common values Civic engagement & participation

Project Objectives

  • Strengthen a sense of European identity among senior citizens and other participants.
  • Encourage seniors' social activation and participation in public life.
  • Develop innovative educational materials about the European Union.
  • Collect and publish seniors' memories of their countries' entry into the EU.
  • Promote dialogue between generations through public debates and workshops.

The Four Partners

The project unites four organisations active in adult education and senior engagement across Europe.

Lead partner

Fundacja "Aktywni XXI"

Jelenia Góra, Poland

Fundacja “Aktywni XXI” is a non-governmental, non-profit organisation based in Jelenia Góra, in the Lower Silesia region (Dolnośląskie). Founded on 6 December 2010, it has worked in adult education for more than ten years, promoting lifelong learning and active participation in social life.

The Foundation was set up to support the creative development of children, young people and adults, with a special focus on older people. It runs the Jelenia Góra Academy of the Third Age, which offers lectures, meetings with interesting people and trips.

Older people are the main target group of the Foundation's educational activities, which also include intergenerational projects. For several years “Aktywni XXI” has worked with the Jelenia Góra branch of the OHP (Ochotnicze Hufce Pracy, Voluntary Labour Corps), organising cultural workshops, Christmas meetings and social initiatives.

The Foundation's mission is to spread the idea of lifelong learning among older people. This called for an attractive educational offer that would draw in new participants and involve them actively. Thanks to the trainers and educators who work with the Foundation, new courses have been created, widening the range of subjects on offer.

The trainers' participation in the course “Theatre in Education: a Holistic Approach to Teaching through Drama Techniques” led to theatre workshops, which are very popular and have been held regularly since February 2022, and to the partnership project “Using Drama Techniques in Adult Education”. Through the project “Social Skills – New Challenges” the trainers attended three courses on social skills; this led to the “Active Citizen” workshops and the courses “A Senior's Life in Harmony Is a Miracle”, on dealing with difficult situations, building relationships and communicating with others. The trainers also designed a board game to develop social and civic skills, which is used in many of the Foundation's activities.

The new teaching methods have increased participation in the Jelenia Góra Academy of the Third Age: the new programmes and more attractive activities have drawn more senior students, as shown by the rise in enrolments in the following semesters.

LUTE Milazzo

Milazzo (ME), Italy

LUTE Milazzo (Free University for the Third Age) is a non-governmental, non-profit association based in Milazzo, Sicily, working in adult education. For many years it has promoted educational, cultural and social activities for the local community, with a special focus on senior citizens.

LUTE is a grassroots branch of the national association AUSER: it works in social promotion and is committed to fostering active ageing and strengthening the role of older people in society.

Although it was created mainly for older people, LUTE is open to people of all ages and pays particular attention to dialogue between generations, nationalities and cultures. It is an association where each person plays a leading role and is a resource for themselves and for others, at any age.

It operates through ten branches in the province of Messina and has about 1,000 members and around a hundred volunteers, who teach courses on subjects ranging from the area's cultural traditions to personal well-being, as well as science and the arts.

LUTE pays great attention to the European dimension of learning and takes part in Erasmus+ projects that link local cultural and craft traditions with European history, using both digital tools and written sources, such as local libraries and the historical memories of its members.

It has taken part in the Erasmus+ projects CCH (Common Cultural Heritage), with Spain and Poland (2019–2022), and CAT (Communication Arts), with Romania and Poland (2022–2024). Its members have repeatedly taken part in European cultural experiences, both individually and as a group.

Seniorenbüro Hamburg e.V.

Hamburg, Germany

Seniorenbüro Hamburg e.V. is an independent, non-profit association based in Hamburg. For many years it has involved senior citizens through volunteering and social participation: it trains volunteers, builds local support networks and runs projects that make the most of older people's skills and experience.

The work of Seniorenbüro is based on the motto “Ageing actively together” and is aimed at people aged 50 and over, whatever their social or cultural background. Because its activities take place in many parts of Hamburg, participants are very diverse; most are between 60 and 85. The association encourages them to play an active part in social life, projects and programmes.

Group activities and courses are easy to join and are designed specifically for people with limited financial means: they are free or low-cost, so everyone can benefit from a wide range of activities.

The main areas of activity:

  1. Promoting volunteering among older people. In courses and one-to-one advice sessions, interested people find out how they can contribute to social and cultural life; volunteers then take part in many projects and programmes.
  2. Open activities for older people. In 5 meeting places and 40 groups across all Hamburg districts, people experience community and active ageing.
  3. Promoting lifelong learning. The meeting places host courses on digital, social and health topics, as well as courses on volunteering. Volunteers receive training on topics related to their work, and specialist events are organised with partners in Hamburg, for example on empowerment and social diversity.

For 25 years Seniorenbüro has supported Hamburg's “Contemporary Witnesses” (Zeitzeugen): a non-partisan group of women and men volunteers who meet regularly to talk about their own experiences and record them in the “Zeitzeugenmagazin”. Their stories deal with historical and social events that are already history for younger generations. Some can still talk about the National Socialist era and how the dictatorship shaped everyday life; others describe the post-war years, divided Germany, social change and technological progress.

The main aim of the contemporary witnesses is to promote peace in a democratic, constitutional state. That is why they talk with young people: they regularly visit schools in Hamburg, share their experiences using photos and documents from the time, and answer pupils' questions. They do not see themselves as historians, but as witnesses who make history understandable and tangible, helping young people understand their country's past so they can make sense of the present and help shape the future responsibly.

Preiļu novada Izglītības pārvalde

Preiļi, Latvia

Preiļu novada Izglītības pārvalde (Preiļi Municipality Education Board) is the local public body that manages education in the Preiļi area, in the Latgale region of Latvia. It coordinates and supports educational development in the municipality, ensuring quality, access and innovation, and works with national and European partners to strengthen adults' skills.

The Board promotes general education and lifelong learning, giving access to all citizens regardless of age, gender, previous education, place of residence, income, ethnic origin or disability. It offers adults high-quality learning that provides useful skills for work, civic participation and personal growth, helping to build a competitive knowledge economy and a democratic society.

The Board runs the Senior Academy, which brings together older people from the municipality to learn new knowledge and skills together, preserve traditions and culture, and promote well-being and a healthy lifestyle. Its main aim is to involve older people actively in cooperation and in the development of the community.

The Board employs 23 people in strategic roles and 25 contract staff. About 350–400 people attend its lifelong learning programmes and about 70 attend the Senior Academy.

The Board has experience in many projects for adults and young people. Its Erasmus+ adult education mobility project “Networking of adult educators and learners in the municipality to improve the quality of teaching and learning through digital learning tools” was named Latvia's best adult education project and was presented at the Lifelong Learning Conference in Brussels (17–18 April 2024). A further project focuses on a sustainable, green lifestyle, shared democratic responsibility and understanding EU values.

What We Do

Workshop Guide

A methodological guide with workshop scenarios on the EU, designed for trainers and educators working with seniors.

Workshops "European ABC"

Educational workshops for senior citizens, covering the history, institutions, values and daily impact of the European Union.

Voices from the Past

A collection of senior citizens' memoirs about their countries' entry into the EU, to share knowledge between generations.

Study Visits

Visits to EU institutions in Brussels and Strasbourg, to experience first-hand where European decisions are made.

Expected Results

  • Greater awareness of the European Union and its values.
  • A stronger sense of shared European identity among senior citizens.
  • Increased civic participation and trust in European institutions.
  • Reduction of Euroscepticism through evidence-based education.
  • High-quality educational materials reusable by other organisations.
